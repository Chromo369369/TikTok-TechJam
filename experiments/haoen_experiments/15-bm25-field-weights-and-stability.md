# BM25 field weights, and a numerical-stability bug caught in the process

The `bm25(products, 0.0, 6.0, 4.0, 2.5, 2.5, 1.5, 1.0)` per-column weights in `_candidate_pool` (title/categories/features/details/store/description) were the original hand-picked baseline values, never revisited despite every downstream layer (the re-ranker, its regularization, its objective) going through multiple tuning rounds. Extracted them into a named `BM25_COLUMN_WEIGHTS` constant and measured how much headroom actually exists before investing in a full retrain cycle for each candidate: instrumented all 200 sessions directly and found the raw BM25 pool (before the exact-phrase-index/category-index union) completely misses the target in 59/200 sessions (29.5%) -- recovered only by that union safety net -- and among the 141 sessions it does find, the median rank is 25 (mean 37.4, worst cases near the 150-item cap). That looked like real headroom.

It mostly wasn't, once measured end-to-end. Three reweighted candidates (`even_ish`: equal 3/3/3/3/2/2 weighting; `boost_desc`: boosting features/details/description relative to title) each required a full re-harvest and re-fit (BM25 weights change pool composition, invalidating any cached feature matrix) and scored 0.951139 / 0.951164 / 0.951462 against the original weighting's own re-fit at 0.951139 -- a spread of about 0.0003, well within what a single fold-assignment reshuffle could produce by chance. The mechanistic reason: the exact-phrase-index and category-index union recall path is already so effective (0/200 sessions ever go fully unrecovered, confirmed both before and after every reweighting tried) that raw BM25 quality has very little marginal effect on the score that actually gets measured -- it mattered a lot for the *isolated* diagnostic and very little for the pipeline as a whole. **Not adopted** -- the gain is indistinguishable from noise and doesn't justify the added re-fit complexity or (see below) the risk of hitting the same instability while re-deriving weights for a changed retrieval configuration.

The more valuable outcome of this investigation was a side-finding, not the BM25 result itself. Re-deriving the listwise model at the documented L2=2.0 on a freshly-harvested (but data-identical) dataset, purely as a sanity check before trusting the BM25 comparison, reproducibly diverged: weight magnitudes past 100-180 on some features, confirmed identical (not a fluke) across two independent re-runs. A follow-up sweep confirmed L2=2.0 sits right at the edge of a genuine instability cliff for this objective -- L2=3.0 and above converge cleanly and smoothly every time, L2=2.0 sometimes does and sometimes (as just demonstrated) doesn't, for reasons not fully isolated. This did **not** affect the already-deployed agent -- `SCORE_WEIGHTS` is a fixed constant baked into `starter/agent.py`, not re-fit at runtime, so the shipped 0.951089 was never at risk -- but it meant the documented L2=2.0 choice was misleading as a *recipe*: rerunning `tools/fit_reranker.py`'s listwise path to reproduce it would not reliably reproduce it. Re-fit at L2=3.0 instead (original BM25 weights, unchanged): **HR@10 1.000, MRR 0.920, technical score 0.951339** -- essentially the same score with a real stability margin -- confirmed by a strict OOF refit at **0.950014**. Deployed in place of the L2=2.0 fit.
